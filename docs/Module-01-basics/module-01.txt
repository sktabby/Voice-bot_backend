Module 1: Audio Fundamentals (Foundation Layer)

1) What is an audio signal?
An audio signal is just a changing value over time that represents sound.
In the real world
Sound = pressure vibrations in air.
Your ear senses how pressure changes over time.
In digital
We store sound as a sequence of numbers over time:
Time on X-axis
Amplitude (loudness/pressure level) on Y-axis
That graph is the waveform.
Key words
Waveform: shape of the signal over time
Amplitude: how “big” the wave is (loudness)
Frequency: how fast it oscillates (pitch)

2) Waveforms, amplitude, frequency (the core trio)
✅ Amplitude (loudness)
Amplitude is the height of the wave.
Bigger amplitude = louder sound
Smaller amplitude = softer sound
Too big = clipping (distortion)
Clipping happens when the wave exceeds the max value the system can store.

✅ Frequency (pitch)
Frequency = how many cycles per second (Hz).
100 Hz = low pitch (bass-like)
1000 Hz = higher pitch
Human speech useful range is mostly ~80 Hz to ~8 kHz (rough intuition)
Speech recognition doesn’t need “music quality”. It needs “speech clarity”.

3) Sample rate (16kHz vs 44.1kHz) — why STT prefers 16kHz
What is sample rate?
Sample rate = how many measurements per second we take from the waveform.
16 kHz = 16,000 samples per second
44.1 kHz = 44,100 samples per second
Why it matters
More samples = more detail = bigger data + more compute.
The big rule (Nyquist)
To represent a frequency of X Hz, you need a sample rate of at least 2X.
So:
16 kHz sample rate can represent frequencies up to ~8 kHz
44.1 kHz can represent up to ~22.05 kHz
Why 16 kHz is enough for STT
Most speech information lives under ~8 kHz.
So 16kHz captures what matters, without extra cost.
That’s why many STT pipelines standardize to:
✅ 16kHz, mono, 16-bit PCM

4) Bit depth (16-bit PCM) — what it really means
What is bit depth?
Bit depth = how precise each sample value is.
Think of it like:
Sample rate = how often you measure
Bit depth = how accurate each measurement is
Common:
16-bit: 65,536 possible levels (from -32768 to +32767)
24-bit: more precision, bigger files
8-bit: low quality
Why 16-bit PCM is standard
Good dynamic range for speech
Compatible with almost all audio tooling + ML pipelines
Keeps file sizes manageable

5) Mono vs Stereo (why mono is enough for speech)
Mono
One channel. Single waveform.
Stereo
Two channels: Left + Right.
Why speech pipelines prefer mono
Speech content is the same; stereo doesn’t add meaning for STT
Stereo doubles data size and compute
Many models are trained on mono
So for STT:
✅ convert stereo → mono early

6) Audio formats: WAV vs MP3/AAC vs Opus
✅ WAV (PCM) — best for ML
Usually raw PCM samples
No compression artifacts
Predictable for models
Used in ML because it’s “clean”.

✅ MP3 / AAC — compressed
These remove information humans don’t notice much, to reduce size.
But STT models may suffer because:
Compression introduces artifacts
Fine details in consonants (“t”, “k”, “s”) can get slightly blurred
Still usable, but:
✅ often you convert MP3/AAC → WAV PCM before STT

✅ Opus — streaming king (WebRTC)
Opus is designed for:
Low-latency streaming
Voice calls
Variable network conditions
Great for transport, but models often want PCM input.
So typical real-time pipeline:
Opus (transport) → decode → PCM → STT

A) Core concepts
Q1. What is an audio waveform?
A: A graph of amplitude (signal value) over time, representing sound.
Q2. Amplitude controls what?
A: Loudness/strength of sound. Too high causes clipping.
Q3. Frequency controls what?
A: Pitch. Higher frequency = higher pitch.

B) Sample rate
Q4. What does 16 kHz mean?
A: 16,000 samples per second.
Q5. Why is Nyquist important?
A: To capture frequency X, sample rate must be at least 2X.
Q6. Why do STT systems often use 16kHz?
A: It captures up to ~8kHz, which contains most speech information, and is efficient.

C) Bit depth
Q7. What does bit depth control?
A: Precision (number of possible amplitude levels per sample).
Q8. Why is 16-bit PCM common in ML?
A: Good balance of quality, compatibility, and file size.

D) Mono vs Stereo
Q9. Why mono is enough for speech recognition?
A: Speech meaning doesn’t require two channels; mono reduces size/compute and matches training.

E) Formats
Q10. Why is WAV PCM preferred for STT?
A: Lossless, predictable, no compression artifacts.
Q11. Why MP3/AAC can slightly hurt STT?
A: Compression artifacts blur details important for recognition.
Q12. Why Opus is popular in calls?
A: Low latency, robust streaming; then you decode to PCM for ML.
